Data flow

How a stick command reaches a joint, and where the current goes. These are classes. No harness. The board on the bench today is an F765-Wing, a P0–P1 bench board with no CAN; the robot's controller is a Teensy 4.1 real-time MCU with an ICM-42688-P IMU and three CAN transceivers (2026-09-26). The four software layers are in docs/software.md.

TBS Nano RXOn hand. CRSF into the Teensy.

Teensy 4.1 + ICM-42688-PThe real-time MCU. Runs the portable control core at 1 kHz: attitude, balance, the four modes, a hardware watchdog. Two classic CAN 1 Mbit buses out: A = two wheels + two hip roll, B = two knees + two hip swing. The F765-Wing is the P0–P1 bench board only (no CAN); its bench plan.

Power

8S 3300 mAh LiPoOne pack, XT90, XT90-S anti-spark on the harness. Decided 2026-09-26: 33.6 V full, 29.6 nominal, 26.4 V cutoff — above the RobStride 24 V floor through the whole discharge.
→
Power split + torque cutActuator current stays on the pack, behind a hardware kill. Do not route motor current through the MCU.
→
Step-down railBucks rated ≥36 V in. 5 V for the Teensy, the receiver and the Pi 5 (5 A); 12–19 V for the companion slot. No servo pose rail unless the servo fallback comes back.

Motion

Three branches leave the Teensy on its two CAN buses. Every actuator runs its own FOC. Actuator current stays on the pack.

Wheel FOC ×2RobStride 05, driver and encoder on the actuator, torque mode on bus A. 5.5 N·m peak, 1.7 rated. 1.5 m/s top (188 rpm at the 6" wheel), 1.0 cruise (R38). Temporary lock, not ordered.
→
In-wheel motors ×2The RS05 sits in the wheel, flush with the tire's outboard face, stator on the axle fitting inboard, disc web to a turned rim (Sheet 2). Not ordered.
Hip roll ×2In V1. RobStride 02 on bus A, not a stepper. With the other wheel up the planted hip holds about 5.2 N·m at the Sheet 1 roll axes (3.0"), vs 7 rated; it was 10.7 at the old 5.4". Temporary lock, not ordered.
Knee and hip swing ×4RobStride 02 at the knee (R39), RobStride 00 at the hip swing, bus B. Temporary lock, not ordered. A knee gravity spring (Sheet 2) carries the two-leg stance — 1.7 N·m at 92%, 2.8 at 75% — so the motor is free to move. Servo or stepper+belt is the fallback only; steppers would sit behind their own drivers, never on the MCU's pins.
