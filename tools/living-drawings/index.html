Hardware / Purchased actuator baseline

What does the motor plug into?

Each Pololu 4752 wheel motor connects to its own Pololu 4035 DRV8874 motor driver through a breakout harness. The Pico 2 tells the driver what to do.

Wiring and pin allocation ↗
  1. The brainPico 2

    3.3 V direction, PWM and gated SLEEP commands.

  2. The power stageDRV8874 driver

    One per wheel. OUT1 / OUT2 carry motor current.

  3. The ordered motorPololu 4752

    Red / black wires drive the motor. Four other wires serve its encoder.

Split the six-wire connector.

The supplied 1×6, 2.54 mm female connector needs a mating breakout or harness. Route motor red/black to the driver; encoder blue/green to 5 V/ground; yellow/white A/B through 5 V → 3.3 V level conversion to the Pico.

Verify the received lead and pinout. Motor power never passes through Pico GPIO or USB. The encoder cannot be powered from 3.3 V under its specified supply range.

Give power its own path.

Bench supply, then the qualified 3S pack → fuse and physical actuator-power cut → driver VIN/GND. Logic gets a separate 5 V branch, with a common signal ground and a defined return-energy path.

One driver is in the Pololu shipment. The second wheel channel, Pico, IMU, harness and power protection still need procurement or inventory confirmation. An arriving motor/driver pair still needs a qualified harness and supply.

The leg servo uses a different interface.

The ordered ST3215 12 V variant has its own internal driver. Pico UART → a compatible 3.3 V half-duplex TTL interface → servo bus. Qualify it on the regulated 9 V servo branch, with a 3:1 belt reduction at each leg.

Two servos are paid; the bus interface is still to confirm. First mobility uses pinned legs; supported servo tests can happen earlier.

Connector specifications: Pololu 4752 · driver terminals: Pololu 4035. This is the connection plan; the measured circuit and final harness remain WIP.

Controls / How Hux reacts

A small, local balance brain.

V1-PROOF will run conventional control on a Pico 2. You will command speed and turns; Hux will continuously move its wheels to keep its body balanced.

Control architecture and modes ↗
01 / Sense

Know the lean and motion.

The LSM6DSOX IMU measures acceleration and rotation. Encoder counts track the wheels. The Pico combines these readings into pitch, pitch rate, position and speed estimates; current, battery and fault inputs constrain movement.

02 / Correct

Keep the wheels under the body.

A target 500 Hz control loop corrects pitch, with slower speed/position correction and differential steering. Both wheels cooperate to balance; their difference turns Hux. Balance gets priority when motor authority is limited.

03 / Supervise

Accept bounded human commands.

A laptop/gamepad or qualified RC link asks for speed and yaw. DISARMED, BALANCE, DRIVE and FAULT modes govern behavior. Stale commands over 250 ms, bad feedback or limit violations latch a fault and require deliberate rearming.

Design locked; implementation ahead. The control architecture and purchased actuator models define V1-PROOF. The host simulation exists; embedded firmware, measured loop timing and physical balance are not implemented or proven. Slow powered height adjustment follows pinned-leg balance and measured trim. The catch fixture contains a power-off fall.

There is no onboard AI, camera or Linux computer in this proof. A future vision/planning computer can request bounded motion; the Pico would retain balance, limits and fault handling. See what the simulation actually establishes ↗
