Side — one leg

Positive hip angle puts the knee to the rear. Knee bend brings the lower tube forward under the hip. Balance is the 92% crouch, hip over the axle, knee behind. Request a wheel position and the hip and knee follow. The red dot is the center of mass, and the dashed line is gravity down to the tread under it. Motor boxes are rough envelopes, not parts.

Top — stance and body

Overall width is 14 inches. The head is the center box. Wheels and legs sit outside it. Track is 12.75 inches with a 1.25 inch tire. The knee folds rearward.

Side — both legs on the stair

Same leg, drawn twice. The left leg is the far one and is lighter. Both hips sit on the head. Request either joint angles or wheel positions; the other follows. Play climbs the stair at real speed (or slower). The shin cannot put the center of mass over the front wheel while the rear wheel is still down, so the rear leg shoves the body forward and the rear wheel leaves with the mass still behind the front contact. Two things get it across: the forward angular momentum from the shove, and the front wheel rolling back in its slot under the mass (the green bracket is the slot, the red bar is how far the wheel has rolled from where it landed). Once the mass is over the contact the wheel absorbs what is left, the body walks forward and stands up, and the trailing foot comes up. Green dots are loaded contacts. The red dot is the center of mass, the red tick its velocity, the dashed arc its angular momentum about the front contact.

Front — sway and roll

The mass sits over the planted wheel whenever the other wheel is off. The 12.75" sway from the trailing wheel to the leading wheel happens during the gather, with both wheels down, and never has to come back: the leading foot is next step's planted foot. Hip axes drawn 5.4" off the centerline (assumption). Numbers at the hips are leg lean from vertical.
