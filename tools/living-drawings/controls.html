SW-01 / Control architecture

Sense, correct, supervise.

V1-PROOF will run conventional control on a Pico 2. You will command speed and turns; Hux will continuously move its wheels to keep its body balanced.

Control architecture and modes ↗
01 / Sense

Know the lean and motion.

The received SparkFun LSM6DSO IMU measures acceleration and rotation. Encoder counts track the wheels. The Pico combines these readings into pitch, pitch rate, position and speed estimates; current, battery and fault inputs constrain movement.

02 / Correct

Keep the wheels under the body.

A target 500 Hz control loop corrects pitch, with slower speed/position correction and differential steering. Both wheels cooperate to balance; their difference turns Hux. Balance gets priority when motor authority is limited.

03 / Supervise

Accept bounded human commands.

A laptop/gamepad or qualified RC link asks for speed and yaw. DISARMED, BALANCE, DRIVE and FAULT modes govern behavior. Stale commands over 250 ms, bad feedback or limit violations latch a fault and require deliberate rearming.

Design locked; implementation ahead. The control architecture and purchased actuator models define V1-PROOF. The host simulation exists; embedded firmware, measured loop timing and physical balance are not implemented or proven. Slow powered height adjustment follows pinned-leg balance and measured trim. The catch fixture contains a power-off fall.

There is no onboard AI, camera or Linux computer in this proof. A future vision/planning computer can request bounded motion; the Pico would retain balance, limits and fault handling. See what the simulation actually establishes ↗
