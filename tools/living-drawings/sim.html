What the robot depends on

Working assumptions, not decisions. Torque limits apply live. Mass, CoM and the skid rebuild the robot where it started.

What this is and is not

  • Physics. Rapier rigid bodies at 2 kHz: body, two hip yokes, four carbon tubes, two wheels, Coulomb friction, real contacts with the stair, sills and crates.
  • Motors. Every joint is a torque with a cap, updated at 2 kHz. The in-wheel motors lose torque linearly to the 4S no-load speed. PARKED shorts the wheel phases (a brake that needs speed to work).
  • Suspension. Each leg is a virtual spring-damper along the hip-to-axle line (3.5 Hz, 0.6 damping by default) with the leg angle held stiff. A real Hux can only do this with torque-controlled, backdrivable joints (FOC / QDD class) or real springs. A position servo or a stepper cannot.
  • Reflexes. A leg that is hit hard at speed hops its wheel up for about a tenth of a second. A wheel stopped dead by an edge at walking pace hops too. When the mass runs away, the legs swing and the hip rolls step the wheels under it. Encoders and the IMU only.
  • One wheel. LEFT / RIGHT ONLY is a planned sequence: stand tall, stiffen, shift the mass over the planted wheel until the other carries about 15%, hold, then centre. Taking the free wheel fully off the floor is the open item; it is behind the experimental switch.
  • Balance. LQR on the lean of the mass over the axle, rebuilt as the legs change height, at 500 Hz. Roll levelling by leg length and leg force. It sees body attitude, joint angles and wheel speed; no sensor noise yet.
  • Tire. Contact is a 6" sphere drawn as a tire. It rolls exactly and matches the stair profile. Sideways it rocks on a 3" radius instead of a ~0.6" crown, so one-wheel balance here is somewhat easier than on the real tire.
  • Not modelled. Motor inertia through the gearbox, backlash, belt stretch, tube flex, battery sag, sensor noise and delay. No stair-climb controller yet: the stair is there to drive up to.

Geometry and lump masses come from kin.js, the same model the drawings use (docs/research/leg-geometry.md). The 3D body is 8" long, 7" wide and 6" above the hips, the same drawing assumptions. The torque limits are classes, not parts. Needs a network connection the first time it opens: three.js and Rapier load from jsDelivr.
